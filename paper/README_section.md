# Unlearning vs. Hiding: Does Unlearning Remove Knowledge or Just Suppress Its Expression?

Lexsi AI Safety and Alignment Hackathon, **Track 2b**, problem statement **7 (Unlearning)**. Model: `CohereLabs/tiny-aya-global` (3.35B), compared with Tiny Aya Earth, Fire and Water (Global with seeds 42, 43 and 44; the others one seed), plus one run of Aya Expanse 8B as a cross-model case. Toolkits chained: **SafeTune** (RMU unlearning), **AuditKIT** (scoring), **CircuitKIT** (EAP-IG circuits; run on Global, not analyzed in this version and left to future work).

> Numbers include the Base unlearn-eval-probes-relearn run and the earlier instruction-tuned and Expanse runs: Global seeds 42, 43 and 44, one seed (42) for Earth, Fire, Water and Expanse, and one Base run, 40 forget questions, small n throughout. Pending: analysis of the head-ablation and circuit artifacts, links and the release tag. No native speaker has checked the translations (a stated limitation). The full paper is `paper/main.tex`.

## Summary

Unlearning should remove designated knowledge, but lower accuracy on direct questions does not show the knowledge is gone. We audit Representation Misdirection for Unlearning (RMU) on Tiny Aya, a 3.35B multilingual family, with SafeTune and AuditKIT. We inject fictitious authors from the Task of Fictitious Unlearning (TOFU) benchmark (`forget01`, 40 pairs) into Tiny Aya Global with LoRA, unlearn with RMU at layer 18 (updating layers 16-18), and repeat on Earth, Fire, Water and Base.

**Method contribution.** Standard RMU did nothing here. A 360-token system preamble, plus attention-sink tokens, dominates the forget loss, which stayed flat near 3.5. Four unmasked settings left forget ROUGE-L (answer overlap) at 0.92-0.97, versus 0.97 injected. Masking the forget loss to first-user-turn tokens (alpha 100, steering coefficient 20, 80 steps) lowers it to 0.32.

**Results.** Judge forget suppression (the fraction of injected facts an output does not reveal) is graded by `qwen3.5:35b` with a seeded 15% Kimi K3 re-grade. On Global (mean of seeds 42-44) it rises from 0.03 to 0.92 on direct questions, 0.33 to 0.95 on paraphrases and 0.08 to 0.95 on extraction prompts, and from 0.71 to 0.93 across eight machine-translated languages. Earth, Fire and Water behave alike (direct 0.875, 0.875 and 0.975 after RMU). Judged retain accuracy falls 2-9 points (Global mean 0.49 to 0.44). Format changes leak: the pooled cloze and true/false bucket reaches only 0.79, driven by true/false (0.70). On Base (not instruction-tuned), a format fix let injection pass (forget ROUGE-L 1.000); the same settings then moved direct suppression only from 0.00 to 0.10 and forget ROUGE-L from 1.000 to 0.913.

**We do not claim erasure.** After 30 fine-tuning steps the RMU model recovers 5, 7 and 7 of 8 forgotten facts across Global seeds 42-44, against 4, 4 and 6 of 8 new facts on the same model and 7, 7 and 4 of 8 for an uninjected base. The gap is one to three facts, too small to call significant. Held-out probes separate forget from retain questions at AUROC 0.92-1.00 from layer 1 up (0.87-1.00 on Base), as does a base model that never saw the facts, so they reflect surface differences, not stored knowledge. On Global (seed 42), a teacher-forced logit lens shows RMU lowers but does not remove the first content token probability (0.971 to 0.696, n=40). We read the results as output-level suppression on the four instruction-tuned variants, with weak evidence of residual knowledge, and as a failure of the same settings on Base. On Aya Expanse 8B the same settings gave direct suppression of only 0.20 and cut judged retain accuracy from 0.91 to 0.67, so we claim no cross-model generality. Judge agreement uses prevalence-robust statistics, since kappa collapses on one-sided labels. Small samples and three Global seeds limit every conclusion. A multilingual unlearning study exists (Farashah et al.); we found no published paper on unlearning Tiny Aya.

## Why the resulting model is meaningfully better for usage

Out of the box, RMU left Tiny Aya Global unchanged (four unmasked settings). With a masked forget loss it suppresses the injected facts under paraphrase, extraction and translation at a measured retain cost of 2 to 9 points. The audit shows what that does not guarantee: format-change leakage remains, and 30 fine-tuning steps recover most forgotten facts. It also does not transfer by default: on Aya Expanse 8B the same settings suppressed direct questions only partly and cost as much on retain facts. We do not claim erasure.

## Contributions, in order

1. **Masked-forget-loss RMU.** The chat template's shared system preamble (about 360 tokens) and attention-sink tokens dominate SafeTune's forget MSE, so unmasked RMU does nothing on Tiny Aya (forget loss flat at about 3.5; best forget ROUGE-L 0.92). Taking the forget MSE only over tokens from the first user turn on fixes it on Tiny Aya (forget ROUGE-L 0.32), with the rest of the SafeTune loop unchanged. The same masked settings only partly worked on Aya Expanse 8B.
2. **One protocol across Tiny Aya variants.** Global (three seeds), Earth, Fire and Water under the same pipeline. An informal web search on 14 November 2026 found no published paper on unlearning Tiny Aya, but it found concurrent, unreviewed hackathon-style repositories that apply RMU (hazardous biology, layers 7 to 9, 4-bit audit) and NPO (TOFU fine-tune, 4-bit compression) to Tiny Aya; this is not a systematic literature search. One cross-model case, Aya Expanse 8B, was run once with the Tiny Aya settings and no tuning.
3. **Multilingual leakage paired with internal tests** (held-out probes with a base-model control, relearning, a teacher-forced logit-lens readout of the answer token, and an activation-patching rerun with controls). Follow-up to Farashah et al. (*Multilingual Amnesia*); the Unlearning Depth Score (Lee et al.) evaluates on TOFU forget10 and does not test other languages or use linear probes.
4. **Mid-depth RMU, relearning and judge-agreement reporting** (raw agreement, kappa, PABAK, Gwet's AC1).

## Setup

- LoRA injection of TOFU `forget01` (each pair shown 4 times per epoch) plus a 400-row `retain99` slice (rank 16, alpha 32, 5 epochs, lr 2e-4); adapter merged before RMU.
- RMU, masked forget loss: steering layer 18, update layers 16 to 18, alpha 100, steering coefficient 20, lr 5e-4, 80 steps.
- Headline metric: judge forget suppression (primary judge `qwen3.5:35b` via Ollama; seeded 15% re-grade by `moonshotai/kimi-k3` via OpenRouter). Missing judgments are counted, not scored as zero.
- Aya Expanse 8B (`CohereLabs/aya-expanse-8b`, about 8B parameters, 2.4 times Tiny Aya's 3.35B; 32 layers): one job with inject, verify, unlearn, eval and relearn (no probes or interpretability). Injection, RMU and evaluation settings are Tiny Aya Global's, carried over unchanged and not tuned (layer 18 of 32 here, 18 of 36 on Tiny Aya; the content mask starts at token 1 here and token 359 on Tiny Aya). It differs only in the model, 20 instead of 40 utility questions, relearning steps 0, 10 and 30, and the omitted stages. No sweep and no secondary judge ran. A host hang interrupted the job during relearning (exit 137, run `20261114T061138Z-inject+verify+unlearn+eval+relearn--6d3be84b`); relearning alone was rerun from the same RMU checkpoint (`20261114T151251Z-relearn-6d3be84b`, exit 0).
- Seeds and runs: Earth, Fire, Water and Expanse ran once (seed 42). Global ran seeds 42, 43 and 44; the seed sets the trainer, the injection shuffle and the RMU sampling, and the judge seed is fixed. Seeds 43 and 44 started from a fresh injection and ran the primary judge only (no agreement statistics), 20 utility questions per set, relearning at steps 0, 10, 30, 3 probe splits and no interpretability stage. Seed 42 reuses the injected checkpoint of an earlier run.
- Formats: direct, paraphrase, cloze, true/false, extraction, and translation into 8 languages.
- Logit lens (Global only): each of the 37 residual positions read through the output head for the injected and RMU checkpoints, 40 forget and 32 retain items, scored on the first content token (openers skipped and teacher-forced into the prompt), 95% Student-t intervals over items.

What the seeds change, plainly: (1) the retain cost is 9.4, 4.7 and 1.6 points (seed 44's drop is one question of 64), so the earlier 3 to 9 point range becomes 1.6 to 9.4; (2) true/false suppression after RMU ranges 0.625 to 0.750, and seed 43's true/false substring recall after RMU is 0.875 (0.65 and 0.60 in seeds 42 and 44); (3) relearning (below). Seed 42 is the lowest on direct, cloze, true/false and pooled suppression.

- **Residual leakage.** Substring recall on cloze plus true/false stays at 0.325 on Global seed 42 (seeds 43 and 44: 0.438, 0.300; Earth 0.300, Fire 0.400, Water 0.300), true/false carries it (cloze recall is 0 after RMU in every run), and the pooled bucket is the weakest format in every run. The base model was not evaluated on this format.
- **Relearning (30 steps, n=8).** Global seed 42: RMU model on forgotten facts 5/8, on new facts 4/8, never-injected base on the forgotten facts 7/8. The RMU-old minus RMU-new gap on the judge is positive in all six runs (seed 42, Earth, Fire, seed 44: one fact; Water: two; seed 43: three), so the earlier one-fact statement holds for four runs and not for Water and seed 43; on substring it is +1, +5, -1, +2, +3, +1 (seed 42, 43, 44, Earth, Fire, Water). The base comparison is mixed: base ahead of the RMU model in seed 42 (7 vs 5) and Fire (8 vs 6), equal in seed 43 and Earth, behind in seed 44 (4 vs 7) and Water (6 vs 7), so the earlier claim that the base learns the facts at least as fast in every variant no longer holds. None of this is significant at n=8, and the runs reuse the same facts. Weak evidence of residual knowledge at best.
- **Probes.** Held-out probes decode at AUROC about 0.98 from layer 1 on Global seed 42 (before RMU). After RMU the frozen-probe AUROC (mean over splits, layers 1 to 36) is 0.949 to 0.993 on that run and 0.920 to 1.000 over all six runs (Global seeds 42, 43, 44, Earth, Fire, Water; per run 0.949-0.993, 0.939-0.997, 0.920-0.996, 0.949-0.996, 0.965-0.996, 0.946-1.000), lowest at layers 22 to 23 and also dipping to 0.909-0.937 before RMU. The base model that never saw the facts does the same (refit probes 0.949 to 1.000 over layers 1 to 36 across the six runs; refit AUROC at layer 1: Earth 0.994, Fire 0.994, Water 0.991, Global seeds 43 and 44 0.994), so they say nothing about stored knowledge.
- **Logit lens (Global; single token, teacher-forced).** The injected and RMU curves coincide through residual 16 by construction (RMU changes no weight below decoder layer 16). The lens is uninformative before the last layers for forget and retain alike (residual 16: forget 0.0023, retain 0.016), so it can neither confirm nor rule out an internal representation. At the output the first-content-token probability goes from 0.971 [0.946, 0.995] (injected) to 0.696 [0.567, 0.824] (RMU, n=40), with the correct token at rank 1 on 30 of 40 items (median rank 1); it is 0.648 [0.502, 0.793] on the 34 judge-suppressed facts and 0.968 on the 6 still matching (n=6, too few to interpret). This fits suppressed rather than erased. The rule skips an opener on 18 of 40 items, the first-answer-token probability is 0.582 [0.442, 0.722], and mean ranks are outlier-driven.
- **Activation patching (Global, appendix; 10 facts about one author).** Injected-model residuals were patched into the RMU model (prefill only, injected tokenizer) at all positions, the last prompt token, or the author-name tokens, over 36 decoder layers (13 for name tokens), scored on the teacher-forced first content token at the final layer as the recovered fraction of the log-probability gap, with 95% t-intervals over facts. Layers 0-15 are exactly 0.000 and all-position patches from decoder layer 18 are 1.000 by construction, as are the controls (inject into inject: mean absolute change 0.0, 10 of 10 within 0.05 nats). The non-trivial results are partial and inconsistent: last prompt token 0.306 [0.122, 0.489] at decoder layer 16, 0.509 [0.266, 0.753] at layer 18, 0.789 [0.598, 0.979] at layer 24; subject tokens 0.601 [0.292, 0.910] at layer 16 and 0.319 [-0.073, 0.711] at layer 18. The last-token value of 0.200 at layer 35 reflects the opener rule. A 6-token prefix match in greedy generations is 9 of 10 for the injected model and 0 of 10 for RMU and for every all-position patch, so there is no full-answer restoration claim.

## Limitations

Few seeds (Global has three, 42 to 44, with no SD or interval reported; every other run is single-seed, and the seeds share the same questions and relearning facts); 40 forget questions; relearning n=8; sweep n=20; hyperparameters set on the evaluated questions; injected (not pretraining) facts, injected only partly on retain (0.47 to 0.52); English-only injection and machine-translated queries (made by the primary judge's own model, `qwen3.5:35b`, and not checked by any native speaker; a review sheet exists but was not completed); model judges that lean lenient on the secondary side, and no second judge for Global seeds 43 and 44; no base-model reference for true/false; Global, Earth, Fire and Water plus a single untuned Aya Expanse 8B run, so no cross-model claim; an informal web search (14 November 2026), not a systematic literature search, supports the statement that no published paper unlearns Tiny Aya; the logit lens covers Global seed 42 only (single token, teacher-forced, uninformative before the last layers); the patching rerun covers 10 facts about one author with a single-token measure and no head- or component-level patching; head ablation and CircuitKIT are not analyzed.

**Tiny Aya Base (diagnosed format bug; RMU then failed).** Base failed the injection gate twice, at forget ROUGE-L recall 0.703 and then 0.729 after doubling epochs (10) and forget repeats (8), against a gate of 0.80. The cause was a prompt-format mismatch in our code, not a failure to learn: Base has no chat template, injection trained on `User: question` / `Assistant: answer` text, but the gate and evaluation fed the bare question. A diagnostic rescoring of the saved second-attempt adapter (no training) gives forget ROUGE-L recall 0.722 with the bare question (18 of 40 at or above 0.80; substring 0.350) and 1.000 in the training format (40 of 40; substring 0.975). The 0.722 comes from a separate single-prompt script and differs by 0.007 from the 0.729 of the run's own gate on the same adapter; we did not trace the difference. A `plain_template` option now applies the training format at injection, evaluation and unlearning, and a rerun with the original recipe (5 epochs, forget repeats 4) passed the gate at forget ROUGE-L recall 1.000 (substring 0.975; retain ROUGE-L 0.751) and then completed unlearning, evaluation, probes and relearning with the same RMU settings as the instruction-tuned variants. Direct judge forget suppression rose only from 0.000 to 0.100 and forget ROUGE-L recall fell only from 1.000 to 0.913, so the corrected run is a failure of that recipe on Base, not a missing result. Base used the primary judge only and had no interpretability stage.

## Ethics

The forget set is synthetic (TOFU fictitious authors); no real personal data and no hazardous-knowledge data are used. We report aggregate recoverability, not a method for extracting private data from deployed systems.
